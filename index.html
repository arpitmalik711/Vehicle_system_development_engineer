// 01 — BACKGROUND

ABOUT ME

I'm a third-year Mechanical Engineering Student at NSUT specialising in analytical vehicle development, system-level validation, and first-principles engineering reasoning.

My work has involved end-to-end vehicle development across electric mobility and off-road systems — from concept architecture and subsystem design to prototype realization, iterative validation, and physical testing.

As the sole mechanical engineering intern at an EV startup, I led the mechanical development of an electric moped from concept to second prototype, contributing to a fleet of 15 operational vehicles currently running in Dwarka, Delhi. My responsibilities included vehicle packaging, structural design decisions, subsystem integration, and iterative prototype refinement under real development constraints.

During my vehicle development internship, I contributed to the complete CAD development of a 4×2 dune buggy platform, focusing on system layout, suspension integration, double wishbone geometry development, and manufacturable packaging.

Most recently, as Chief Design Engineer of Team Daedalus Racing, I led the complete development of a BAJA SAE vehicle from scratch. Working closely with driver feedback and CAE validation, I developed the full chassis architecture and vehicle packaging within rulebook constraints using SOLIDWORKS, conducted roll cage ergonomic studies in CATIA, and designed suspension geometry around real vehicle-performance objectives such as improved approach angle, increased ground clearance, and better obstacle handling.

The suspension system was iteratively refined through digital validation in Lotus Shark and months of workshop-floor iteration and physical testing, translating analytical design intent into validated vehicle behavior. This development effort contributed to securing AIR 2 Overall at ATVC 2026.

More recently, my work has expanded into EV subsystem R&D involving swappable battery systems, thermal safety considerations, and integration-focused mechanical development for electric mobility platforms.

My engineering approach begins with understanding why a system behaves the way it does before validating solutions through geometry, analysis, simulation, and physical reasoning. I document my work as engineering case studies that capture not only final outcomes, but the design decisions, trade-offs, iterations, and engineering intuition that shaped them.

— 01 / 02
Chassis Member Failure at Damper Mount

High-G landing at Baja SAE 2025 exposed a critical load-path error at the damper mount. Combined bending + axial + twisting stresses confirmed failure analytically (σVM = 1136 MPa, FOS 0.67) and via FEA (831 MPa, FOS 0.92). Resolved through triangulated load-path redesign — post-fix FEA: 555 MPa, FOS 1.37.

— 02 / 02
Mahindra 265 Steering Geometry Investigation

Why does a 2013 production tractor run positive camber? A childhood observation turned into a full reverse-engineering investigation — KPI measurement, SolidWorks reconstruction, Ackermann verification, and scrub radius analysis to decode the engineering intent behind +3° camber on the front axle.

INVESTIGATION PROGRESS 85%
✓
Measured KPI (≈7.5°) and positive camber (≈+3°) on 2013 Mahindra 265; identified kingpin–tyre clearance constraint via physical inspection
✓
Full front axle and steering assembly reverse-engineered in SolidWorks; KPI, camber, steering arm angles, and pivot geometry faithfully recreated
✓
Ackermann geometry verified by extending steering arm projections toward rear axle in CAD
✓
Inner/outer steering angles recorded experimentally using smartphones mounted on front wheels; behavior validated against CAD model
✓
Scrub radius calculated from CAD; hypothesis developed — positive camber provides kingpin clearance while maintaining a controlled scrub radius
✓
Comparison reference collected from 2005 Mahindra 265 — appears to carry little or no positive camber; comparison analysis planned
